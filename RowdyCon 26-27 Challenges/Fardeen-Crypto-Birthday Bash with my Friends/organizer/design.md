## Fixed cryptographic contract

Alphabet A–Z, A=0; punctuation passes unchanged and does not advance the schedule. Initial primer length 16. For i≥16, C[i]=P[i]+P[i−16] modulo 26. A known message letter propagates forward and backward along its residue class. Target 256 letters; greeting occupies zero-based positions 0–9, closing 247–255. They cover all sixteen residue classes with three independent overlaps. Greeting alone determines 160 letters; closing alone 144. This is an algebraic fact, not a claim that language cannot supply other cribs. Accept equivalent external solutions.

The flag begins at letter 129 (zero-based 128), so its known RCC prefix adds no new chains to the greeting. The 29 flag letters include a fixed 17-letter unpredictable suffix spanning all sixteen classes; partial English recovery cannot simply guess that suffix. Whitespace and flag punctuation remain visible, potentially enabling alternative reasoning. Do not block those alternatives. The target is the same on every reset, copy, and deployment. The sample has its own distinct fixed key. There is no target key reuse with another encrypted note.

The pure client calculator receives ciphertext plus player assumptions; no target primer, target plaintext, expected hash, or flag is shipped in public executable files. The entire memo is calculated, including the flag. A full sheet is labelled filled, not verified correct. A minimal server endpoint checks the exact recovered flag against a private SHA-256 digest stored outside the public tree, returning only valid/invalid. Only a verified actual flag earns the original children’s “Yay!” and the recovered status. A wrong complete starter may fill the sheet but cannot trigger that reward. Plaintext, downloads, and external solving remain independent of the celebration endpoint.

## State and ordinary operation

The target initially has independent rows: a plaintext pin derives only its corresponding key letter. The player must infer the feedback relationship from the postcard and select **Key ribbon: Copy message**, **Offset: 16**. Generic alternatives include no link and encoded-text copying, with offsets from 1 to 64. Each choice calculates its declared relationship; no recognized setting unlocks stored content. The sample derives its full key independently from its known pair and does not label the feedback source.

Immutable public corpus; local reversible pins, starter assumptions, and ribbon links. Derived letters keep origin metadata. Disagreements remain visible without silently overwriting a chain. Undo/redo, reset confirmation, persistent draft, downloadable/restorable working copy, actual text downloads, copy fallback, modal focus restoration, keyboard controls, minimize/restore/maximize, help, and sound/volume operate on the same state. Ribbon rebuilding restores the focused row and absolute letter only when focus was already inside that ribbon.

Authentic short FNAF recordings replace the synthetic cues. A first-time visitor starts with sound enabled at 35%, with lower per-cue gains. Balloon Boy’s initial “Hello” waits for the first browser-permitted interaction; saved mute or zero volume is honored, retaining an unheard first greeting until sound is explicitly enabled. Once activated, the page also attempts an ambient Hello at random 45–100 second intervals, only while visible, unmuted and above zero volume. The user's explicit random-greeting request is the sole timing exception; all puzzle data remains fixed. Busy audio skips that attempt without queuing it. Hiding the page, muting, zero volume or page departure cancels the timer and stops an ambient greeting; returning starts a fresh wait. Page restoration resumes scheduling, and final navigation removes lifecycle listeners. Original camera-flip audio accompanies documents, postcard changes, pins, conflicts, clears, and windows with restrained levels. The two mirror camera files are identical recordings, not distinct sounds. One media element prevents overlap; generic rapid events are coalesced, and greeting/cheer are protected from unrelated interruptions. Mute stops playback immediately. Reload does not replay an already-heard initial greeting or an acknowledged celebration; ambient greetings still wait for an interaction and their own interval. There are no background loops or jump scares.
